# Product

## Register

product

## Users

**Baristas** — primary daily users of the POS surface. Work on touchscreens during service. Need speed, clarity, and reliability under pressure. Login via PIN. Take orders, customize items, process payments. Not technical — the UI must be self-evident.

**Managers & Owners** — secondary users of the admin dashboard. Manage menu, inventory, employees, expenses, discounts, and settings. Review analytics/reports. Work on desktop or tablet. Need an overview of operations at a glance and the ability to drill down without friction.

Both groups operate in a single physical cafe in the Cambodian market. English-first UI for now; KHR currency configuration exists as a setting.

## Product Purpose

Big Brew is a single-store POS + operations management system purpose-built for independent craft coffee shops. It replaces paper tickets, spreadsheets, and disconnected tools with one integrated system: baristas take orders on a touch-optimized POS, managers run the business from an admin dashboard, and everything shares one database.

Success is a system that baristas can learn in one shift and managers can trust for accurate daily reporting — no training manual required.

## Brand Personality

Warm, grounded, efficient.

- **Warm** — the coffee shop's character shows through color and material, not decoration or illustration. Inviting without being cute.
- **Grounded** — sturdy, reliable, present. Like a well-worn espresso machine. No trend-chasing, no unnecessary flourishes.
- **Efficient** — respects the user's time and attention. Every interaction has purpose. Speed is a feature; the UI gets out of the way.

## Anti-references

- **Fast-food / chain** — no bright primary colors, glossy surfaces, or plastic feel. Big Brew is craft coffee, not a drive-through.
- **Over-designed / trendy** — no glassmorphism, gradient text, heavy shadows, decorative animations, or "designed" gestures. Function determines form.
- **Dark-and-edgy** — no dark theme as default, no moody or industrial vibe. The tone is warm and welcoming, not atmospheric or dramatic.

## Design Principles

1. **Speed is the feature.** The POS is used in high-volume service. Every millisecond of UI latency is friction. Render fast, respond instantly, animate only when it aids comprehension (not decoration). A barista under pressure should never wait for the interface.

2. **Warmth without decoration.** The coffee-shop identity lives in the color palette and material — rich browns, warm cream, amber accents — not in illustrations, decorative borders, or thematic flourishes. Visual interest comes from depth, texture (grain overlay), and intentional use of space.

3. **Clarity over cleverness.** Baristas and managers scan the UI under real time pressure. Every label, icon, and layout choice must be immediately legible. No ambiguous icons, no hidden gestures, no animation that delays an action. If someone hesitates, it's a design failure.

4. **Touch-first, all-day comfortable.** The POS is used on touchscreens for entire shifts. Interactive targets are large (minimum 44px), spacing is generous, and the interface reduces visual noise so fatigue stays low. The admin side is mouse-first but lives in the same visual system.

5. **Craft-coffee, not fast-food.** The brand evokes an independent neighborhood cafe — warm and genuine — not a chain. Colors are earth-toned and restrained. Forms are simple and honest. The system feels hand-crafted, not templated.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Follow standard best practices targeting WCAG 2.1 AA level as a baseline: minimum 4.5:1 contrast for body text, 3:1 for large text, keyboard-navigable UI, visible focus indicators, and support for screen readers on actionable elements. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` across all animations.
